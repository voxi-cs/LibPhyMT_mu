Linear Equation

We are somewhat familiar with linear equations of the form \(2x = 6\), or \(2x + 3y = 5\), or maybe \(x + 2y + 7z = 12\). These are examples of linear equations. Usually, we use them to describe a system, and they hold valuable information in their structure. The \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\) are unknowns in these equations. The unknowns in a linear equation may represent many things, such as populations in different cities, volumes of different systems, or some other quantity. The general definition of a linear equation is:

Definition: Linear Equation A linear equation is a specific type of equation that has the following general form:

\[ c_1x_1 + c_2x_2 + c_3x_3 + \dots + c_ix_i + \dots + c_nx_n = b \]

Where \(c_1, c_2, \dots, c_i, \dots, c_n\) are called the coefficients, and the variables \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_i, \dots, x_n\) are the unknowns. The constant \(b\) is referred to as the constant term.

We are very much familiar with linear equations. For example: \[ 3x + 4y = 0 \] is a linear equation, so is: \[ x + y + z = 0 \] but the equation \[ 6xy + 28z + 496y = 0 \] is not a linear equation because of the \(xy\) term. Nor is the equation: \[ 2x^2 + y + 3z = 8 \] A linear equation has degree one, meaning every variable has a power of at most one, and no variable is multiplied or divided by another variable in the same term.

We can graph our linear equations as long as they have at most 3 unknowns. More than that, we cannot visualize the dimension, let alone the hyperplane that they produce. Below, we show how various linear equations with two or three unknowns produce a line or a plane.

System of Linear Equations

We told you about linear equations, but a system of linear equations is equally simple. What a system of equations tells us is how the unknowns are related in different ways, allowing us to narrow down to a simple, singular solution, assuming there is just a single solution for the unknowns.

A system of linear equations is defined as:

Definition: System Of Linear Equations A set or collection of linear equations is called a system of linear equations. For \(m\) equations with \(n\) unknowns, a system of linear equations can generally be written as:

\[ \text{System of Linear Equations}\quad \begin{cases} c_{11}x_1 + c_{12}x_2 + \cdots + c_{1n}x_n = b_1, \\ c_{21}x_1 + c_{22}x_2 + \cdots + c_{2n}x_n = b_2, \\ \vdots \\ c_{m1}x_1 + c_{m2}x_2 + \cdots + c_{mn}x_n = b_m. \end{cases} \]

Having this system of equations, we would like to solve it. Even if we can't solve a system of linear equations, we can still get insight into the system that these equations describe, but here we will talk about solving the system of linear equations.

Solving a System of Linear Equations

Before we jump deep, let's start with a simple exercise. Given the system of linear equations below, let's solve for \(x\) and \(y\). \[ 6x+y=6 \] \[ 2x+3y=6 \]

We can easily solve them using the method of substitution, but we can solve them if we just plot them:

We see that the intersection of the two lines defined by the equations gives us the solution to the system of linear equations. There is another way to make sense of the solution of a system of linear equations, and that is by using column vectors.

We have the system of linear equations: \[ 6x+y=6 \] \[ 2x+3y=6 \] Look carefully: we can turn this into a linear combination as below: \[ x\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2\end{pmatrix} + y \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 6\end{pmatrix} \] This linear combination asks a kind of different question from the structure of the system of linear equations we are familiar with. This form asks the question: for what specific values of the coefficients (of the linear combination) does the linear combination produce a certain vector? And it is rather easier to visualize the question that it poses geometrically.

The exact vector construction of the solution of the system of linear equations is:

This way of picturing the solution of a system of linear equations is called the column picture, and it is a very useful thing to know about.

Similarly, in the case of three vectors, it is the same reasoning and poses the same question, and so on for any number of vectors. It is so much easier to think of \(n\)-dimensional vectors being linearly combined than imagining hyperplanes in an \(n\)-dimensional space intersecting at a particular point, like a coordinate, which is our solution. We will make use of the geometrical and mathematical advantages of vectors to solve systems of equations or simplify the systems of equations in ways that will allow us to solve them much more easily than in their general form.

Soon, when we study Linear Transformation, you will see once again that our way of asking the question to find a solution to the system of linear equations changes. There, our question will change from the linear combination to the question: "Under the Linear Transformation by the transformation matrix \(\mathbf{T}\), which vector \(\mathbf{x}\) lands on the vector \(\mathbf{b}\)?" Which is a much more interesting way of looking at a system of linear equations.

It's a beautiful thing how we can look at a simple problem from different directions and explore it and figure things out with no restriction, but with the limitations that we may have. The only thing we can do is keep exploring, hoping for a lead. Even if everything turns void, it's important to keep exploring and keep looking from different angles, till the void shatters and crumbles to pieces, leaving nothing except pure light.

Our linear combination form of the simple system of linear equations: \[ x\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2\end{pmatrix} + y \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 6\end{pmatrix} \] can be compressed further. Look carefully: it's just a matrix multiplication: \[ \begin{pmatrix} 6 & 1 \\ 2 & 3\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 6\end{pmatrix} \] Here, the matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 1 \\ 2 & 3\end{pmatrix}\) is what we call a coefficient matrix, because it contains the coefficients. This equation, for the general case of any system of linear equations, can be written as: \[ \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b} \] \[ \begin{pmatrix} c_{11} & c_{12} & \cdots & c_{1n} \\ c_{21} & c_{22} & \cdots & c_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ c_{m1} & c_{m2} & \cdots & c_{mn} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \dots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ \dots \\ b_m \end{pmatrix} \] Where \[ \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} c_{11} & c_{12} & \cdots & c_{1n} \\ c_{21} & c_{22} & \cdots & c_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ c_{m1} & c_{m2} & \cdots & c_{mn} \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \dots \\ x_n \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ \dots \\ b_m \end{pmatrix}. \] If any of this confuses you, go back to the definition of a system of linear equations. In the equation, the vector \(\mathbf{b}\) contains the right-hand sides of the system of linear equations in a vector accordingly. It may sometimes appear that we write such a system of linear equations in the form: \[ \left( \begin{array}{cccc|c} c_{11} & c_{12} & \cdots & c_{1n} & b_1 \\ c_{21} & c_{22} & \cdots & c_{2n} & b_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ c_{m1} & c_{m2} & \cdots & c_{mn} & b_m \end{array} \right) = \left(\mathbf{A}\mid\mathbf{b}\right) \] They are essentially the same thing, but in different forms, carrying the same necessary information that is truly important to solve for the unknowns.

Alright, but what is the point of any of this? Why are we doing this? Why should we care about the matrices? And why in this form? Well, now we will explore the idea of solving the system of linear equations using the method of elimination. For this method, the matrix is an essential tool that we must have.

Remember, the only time we will have exactly one set of values as a solution to a system of linear equations is not necessarily when \(m=n\). However, in the special case \(m=n\), where the number of variables is equal to the number of equations, we may have a unique solution, provided the coefficient matrix is invertible. This is just a reminder; we will explore these ideas more carefully as we continue.
